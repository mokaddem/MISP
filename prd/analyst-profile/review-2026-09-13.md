# Read-back — the Assessment tab, against the instance it runs on

**Date: 2026-09-13. Scope: the live Assessment tab — `ValuesController::
viewVerdict`, `viewVerdictAside`, `viewVerdictCard`, the seventeen
`value_verdict_*.ctp` templates, `ValueVerdictTool`, `ValueLeanTool`,
`ValueContestedTool`, `ValueSummaryTool`, `ValueLeanReasonTool` and the
eleven shipped signals — as of `be5afe69a`.** The brief was to check that
what the tab renders is correct, accurate, complete and meaningful,
against real rows rather than against the fixture.

Method: every panel fetched over HTTP as an authenticated reader, every
number on the page re-summed, and every fact it states traced back to the
database it came from. Where one value could not settle a question, the
engine was run over a sample — `12-assessment-audit-probe.php`, 120
values with 2 to 30 events each.

## Verdict

**The arithmetic is exact and the facts are true.** The exact-sum
invariant holds on every value checked, in both layouts: `8.8.8.8` closes
at 70 and 71 against six and four rows, `4d9a0bcd…` at 19 against eight,
`127.0.0.1` at 20 against eight, `23.227.38.32` at 56 and 11. The rail's
per-panel regrouping reconciles to the same totals. Every claim traced to
rows matched them — occurrence and organisation counts, *5 of 5 events
are published*, *1 of 5 occurrences set first_seen*, sighting counts and
their organisations, the TTL bucket, the expiry date and the days over.
The relevance axis is the strongest work on the page: derived end to end,
and it states what it cannot measure rather than guessing (*"Nothing
records when this value was seen — only when its rows were last
written. Its age is a minimum, not a measurement."*). `ValueChangersTool`
was spot-checked against the thresholds it predicts and is right. The
89 assertions in `10-wiring-http-probe.sh` pass.

**What is wrong is what the numbers are labelled as**, and it is one
decision rather than a list of bugs. D11 §2.4's anchoring — `row = points
× polarity` — is applied to the whole catalogue, including the signals
that grade the record rather than read it. The decision was sound when it
was taken and the same rework that took it removed its counterweight
(§A1). Four of the findings below are that one fact seen from four
angles; the rest are mechanical.

On the instance, **50% of sampled values render `Contested`, and 92% of
those reach it through a rule that does not mean the record contradicts
itself** (§A2). The page then builds a *"Reads as benign"* case out of
five absences and declares it the winner over a threat case on a value
where zero organisations said harmless (§A3). That is the defect to fix
before anything else, and §D proposes the one change that closes all
four.

**Why no probe caught it.** `8.8.8.8` is genuinely contested — a
false-positive listing against eight asserting organisations, decided by
an escalation rule — so every phase artefact that used it as *the*
contested case passed, correctly. The question none of them asked was how
the **other** contested values got there.

## A. The anchoring, and what it does to real rows

### A1. The polarity flip is applied to signals that have no polarity

D11 §2.4 anchors every ledger row to the lean at assembly:
`row = points × polarity`, quality is the anchored sum, `direction` is
the row's own sign. On a `threat` lean the polarity is `+1` and nothing
moves. On a `benign` lean it is `−1` and the entire catalogue inverts.

`127.0.0.1`, rendered live, lean `benign`, quality 20:

| row | as the page shows it |
|---|---|
| 4 independent organisations reported it | **−28** ▼ disputes benign |
| Present in 1 enabled feed | **−4** ▼ |
| 3 of 4 events are published | **−7** ▼ |
| Nobody has sighted this value | **+4** ▲ supports benign |
| No galaxy on any occurrence | **+7** ▲ |
| The record never says when it was seen | **+6** ▲ |
| Last reported 17 months ago | **+4** ▲ |
| Hits a known-benign warninglist | **+38** ▲ |

The ledger column header carries the honest gloss — *"Points for and
against the stated lean, not for and against maliciousness"* — and the
rows underneath it are still not defensible. Four organisations
corroborating a value is not an argument against it being benign. Nobody
having sighted it is not an argument that it is. A record failing to date
its own observations is not an argument for anything; it is the
definition of a weak record, which is what `RecordTemporalPrecision`'s
own docblock says it measures.

One row in eight is a genuine benign argument. The other seven are the
quality catalogue, sign-flipped, and the number they sum to is what the
hero's tooltip calls *"How strongly the record supports Asserted
benign"*. It is not measuring that. On a `benign` lean, quality now
measures **how empty the record is**, which is the exact inverse of D11
§2.3's definition — *"how much corroborated, attributed, temporally
precise weight stands behind the record"*.

**Why it broke, precisely.** §2.4 recorded three things carrying the
decision, and the first was that *"the arithmetic is unchanged — the
benign demo value's quality is the same 91, its rows flipped to match the
`with`/`against` arrows the fixture already renders"*. That is true of
the fixture, and the fixture's benign ledger
(`ValueProfileFixture::benignLedger()`, line 3642) contains two rows the
live catalogue no longer has:

```
+13  8 of 9 occurrences set to_ids = no
+16  Decayed under both models (4/100 and 0/100)
```

`−11 +13 +26 −4 +7 +38 +16 +6 = 91`. Those `+29` are what carried a
benign quality into positive territory. D11 §6 then promoted the first
out of the catalogue into the lean derivation (*"`reporting.to_ids_stance`
is promoted out of the catalogue into the lean derivation"*) and moved
the second into the relevance axis with no ledger points at all (*"Same
TTL, clock, runway, honest states; no ledger points"*).

**The flip stayed; the two rows that justified it left in the same
rework.** What remains under a benign lean is nine corroboration and
completeness signals with their signs reversed, plus the warninglist and
false-positive rows. That is the whole finding, and A2 to A4 are its
consequences.

*Fixed 2026-09-13 — §D1 as specified. `ValueSignalBase` gains
`AXIS_LEAN` / `AXIS_QUALITY` and a per-row override; `ValueVerdictTool
::anchor()` multiplies by the polarity only on the lean axis.
`127.0.0.1` now reads `+28` for four organisations reporting it and
bands `low` on 18 rather than 20 — the same rows, none of them
inverted. The two signals that declare `AXIS_LEAN` are
`lifecycle.warninglist` and `sightings.false_positive`, which is D11
§2.1's list minus the `to_ids` stance; the warninglist's `no_hit` pole
declares `AXIS_QUALITY` per row, because* nothing matched, 8 lists
checked *is the control case and anchoring it was enough on its own to
tip an uncontested benign value into rule 7.*

### A2. Rule 7 fires on thin records, not on contradictory ones

`ValueVerdictTool` line 263: a ledger that sums below zero against its
anchor is re-anchored threat-signed and the lean becomes `contested`.
The docblock's premise is that *"a negative quality means the ledger, row
by row, disputes the assertion the record itself makes"*. Under A1 that
premise holds only when the negative sum came from benign-side evidence.
When it comes from absence penalties — no galaxy `−7`, no first-seen
`−6`, no sighting `−4`, not recent `−4`, no feed `−2` — a thin record
trips the rule with nothing disputing anything.

The probe, over 120 values:

```
--- lean exits (decided_by -> final lean)
  threat_supermajority -> threat                 60
  threat_supermajority -> contested              31
  benign_supermajority -> contested              24
  escalation           -> contested               4
  no_supermajority     -> contested               1
```

**60 of 120 values render contested. Five of those sixty mean it** — four
by an escalation rule, one because neither side reached the
supermajority. The other 55 are rule 7 acting on a record that is thin
rather than self-contradictory.

This is the state D11 §4 promised the three-axis model would separate.
*"UNKNOWN's two meanings separate naturally: no record is lean `none`;
thin, inconclusive record is a lean with low quality and a full
ledger."* Rule 7 is what prevents the second half of that sentence from
ever happening: the thin record does not get a lean with low quality, it
gets `contested`.

*Fixed 2026-09-13 — rule 7 weighs `lean_weight`, the anchored sum of
the lean rows, and defers to a lean an escalation already settled. The
same 120 values, re-measured:*

```
--- lean exits (decided_by -> final lean)
  threat_supermajority -> threat                 90
  benign_supermajority -> benign                 24
  escalation           -> contested               4
  lean_disputed        -> contested               1
  no_supermajority     -> contested               1
```

*60 contested became 6, and all six mean it — four escalations, one
split with no supermajority, and `2.2.2.2`, where five organisations
assert a threat over two false-positive sightings. `awake-weaves.cyou`
now reads* **Asserted threat · Quality low · thin record**, *which is
D11 §3's first test reading verbatim.*

### A3. The contested cases are built from the sign alone, so absences become arguments

`ValueContestedTool::casesFor()` splits the ledger on the sign of each
contribution and titles the two halves *Reads as a threat* and *Reads as
benign*. Its docblock states the assumption plainly — *"the positive rows
are what says this is a threat and the negative rows are what says it is
not"* — which is A1's premise again, and fails the same way.

`awake-weaves.cyou`, a `.cyou` domain abuse.ch reported across 11
published events with `to_ids` set, renders:

- badge **Contested**
- lean band: *"1 organisation asserts a threat · 0 report it as
  harmless"*, and under it *"1 of 1 organisation asserts this is a
  threat, at or past this profile's supermajority of 66%."*
- **Reads as a threat — 22**: 11 of 11 events published (9), 1
  organisation reported it (7), no warninglist hit (6)
- **Reads as benign — 23**: no galaxy (7), the record never says when it
  was seen (6), nobody has sighted it (4), last reported 20 months ago
  (4), no enabled feed carries it (2)

Five absences, none of them evidence of benignity, presented as a case
and shown winning 23 to 22 on a value nobody has called harmless.

The same fault with the sign the other way, on `23.227.38.32` (a Shopify
address, 3 of 4 organisations reporting it without the indicator flag):
the *Reads as a threat* case totals 56 and includes **"The record dates
its own observations — 1 of 5 occurrences set first_seen"** at `+4`. A
temporal-precision credit is rendered as a reason to read the value as a
threat.

Every one of the 60 contested values in the sample carries at least one
quality-only signal seated in a case that claims it is a reading. The
signals concerned: `reporting.independent_orgs`,
`reporting.published_ratio`, `lifecycle.feeds`, `lifecycle.recency`,
`lifecycle.continuity`, `sightings.volume_recency`,
`record.temporal_precision`.

The one row where the split is defensible is `attribution.galaxy`, whose
docblock argues the absence case explicitly — *"nine occurrences, four
organisations, and nobody willing to attribute it to anything is evidence
rather than a gap"*. That argument is about a **well-populated** record;
it does not carry to `awake-weaves.cyou`, where nobody attributed it
because almost nobody touched it. D11 §2.3 settles it anyway:
*attribution* is named there, among quality's sources, and nowhere in
§2.1's list of the lean's.

*Fixed 2026-09-13 — `ValueContestedTool::rowsOf()` reads `lean_ledger`
instead of splitting the quality ledger by sign. On the shipped
catalogue both lean signals argue benign, so no value has a second
column and `casesFor()` answers empty — which the page already handles:
`ValueLean::hasConflictedLayout()` sends it to the agreeing layout,
where the contradiction is stated in the lean band with its weight.
The two-column layout stays for a profile whose catalogue carries a
lean signal arguing the other way. Probe: 0 of the 6 contested values
seats a quality signal in a case.*

### A4. Rule 7 rewrites the lean and not the reason, so the band contradicts the badge

`decided_by` is set by `ValueLeanTool` before any scoring and is not
touched when rule 7 flips the lean. `ValueLeanReasonTool` reads it. Rule
7 has no prose of its own, so the band prints the sentence for the lean
that was just discarded.

`icanhazip.com`, live:

> **Contested**
> How this reading was decided
> 0 organisations assert a threat · 2 report it as harmless
> *2 of 2 organisations report this as harmless, at or past this
> profile's supermajority of 66%.*

The badge and the band under the heading *How this reading was decided*
describe two different readings, and the band describes the one the page
is not showing. This is the state of 55 of the 60 contested values.

`ValueLeanTool::leanFor()` has seven exits and `ValueLeanReasonTool`
writes a sentence for five of them — including `no_supermajority`, which
is already the right sentence for a record that contradicts itself. Rule
7 is the eighth exit, it is the one that produces most of the contested
values on this instance, and it is the one with no sentence.

*Fixed 2026-09-13 — §D2. Rule 7 writes `decided_by = 'lean_disputed'`,
and `ValueLeanReasonTool` gains the eighth case: it names the counted
lean, because that half is still true and is what the stance bar above
is drawing —* "The organisations assert this is a threat, and the
record's own evidence about the value disputes them — so the record
contradicts itself." *It does not overwrite an escalation's own prose:
rule 7 defers to a lean already contested, which is how `8.8.8.8` keeps
citing the rule that decided it.*

## B. Contradictions between two surfaces of the same page

### B1. The tab pill and the Overview card print a number the tab says cannot exist

`value_verdict_case_composition.ctp` line 135, in the rail, under the two
case totals:

> *The two totals are never added or subtracted. A single number would be
> the mean of two incompatible readings.*

`$verdict['quality']` on a contested value **is** their difference —
rule 7 re-anchors and re-sums, which arrives at the same number. And it
is printed, twice, outside the rail:

- the tab strip (`Values/view.ctp` lines 410–412) reads **`Contested 45`** for
  `23.227.38.32` (56 − 11) and **`Contested -1`** for `8.8.8.8`
  (70 − 71);
- the Overview card prints a bare **`-1`** under the word *Quality*.

The hero's own sentence is banded off the same difference, which is how
`8.8.8.8` — eight organisations, 53 sightings, 20 events, a warninglist
hit and a feed — comes to read *"The record behind that is thin."*

Either the totals combine and the rail should stop saying they do not, or
they do not and the two surfaces printing their difference should stop.
Under §D the question mostly dissolves: a contested lean would become
rare and would mean what it says.

*Dissolved 2026-09-13 by §D1. The quality is the sum of the quality
rows and the two case totals fold out of the lean ledger, so the
difference of the cases is `lean_weight` and not the quality — the
claim on the composition card is now simply true. `8.8.8.8` reads*
**Contested · Quality medium · 57** *where it read `−1` and* "the
record behind that is thin"; *the tab pill reads `Contested 57`. A
negative quality is still reachable on a very thin record —
`awake-weaves.cyou` closes at `−1` — but it no longer means anything
beyond* the absences outweighed the record, *which is what the `low`
band beside it says. Whether the shipped weights should floor there is
a calibration question and the profile's to answer; see §F.*

### B2. `Reads the value as` is an empty column, and it is the one the layout needs

`value_verdict_conflicted.ctp` line 300 passes `'orgColumns' =>
array('reads')` unconditionally. Nothing produces `org['reads']` — it is
a fixture-only key, and `ValueProfile::verdictOrgTable()`'s docblock
(line 13037) says so and says why:

> *"`reads` is left unset on purpose. `value_verdict.ctp` adds the column
> only when some organisation carries it, so an absent key removes a
> column rather than emptying one."*

The agreeing layout honours that. The contested one renders the header
and an empty `<span>` in every row, and emits `Undefined array key
"reads"` at `value_verdict_orgs.ctp` line 106 once per organisation —
**543 of them in `error.log`**, the most frequent warning on the
instance.

The cost is not only the noise. The contested layout passes *only*
`reads`, so it drops `to_ids` and `reliability` — the per-organisation
stance and the trust grade. On the one layout whose subject is
disagreement between organisations, the two columns that would show it
are replaced by a column that shows nothing.

*Fixed 2026-09-13 — the contested layout asks for `to_ids` and
`reliability`, the two columns that carry the disagreement, and
`value_verdict_orgs.ctp` guards `reads` with `??` so a profile that
does produce it cannot raise a notice either. No `Undefined array key
"reads"` since.*

### C2. The falsifiability card offers actions the exclusions have already ruled out

Same value. `sightings.volume_recency` and `sightings.false_positive` are
in `not_counted` because the value is over-correlating, and *What would

> *A second source — one more organisation reporting it, **or one sighting

A sighting cannot move that value's quality, because the signal that

*Fixed 2026-09-13 — two guards, because there were two ways to offer a

`213.205.40.169` now reads* "A second source — one more organisation
reporting it." *with the sighting half dropped.*

## D. The fix

### D1. Quality signals are unsigned; only lean signals anchor

The three-axis model's whole claim is that lean and quality are different
questions. The anchoring re-fuses them, because it treats every row as a
statement about the lean. Three of the eleven shipped signals are:

| signal | what it reads |
|---|---|
| `lifecycle.warninglist` | the listing's category — a lean input per D11 §2.1 |
| `sightings.false_positive` | false-positive evidence — a lean input |
| `attribution.galaxy` | arguable; the absence case is authored as a lean input, the presence case is corroboration |

The other eight measure the record: corroboration breadth, published
ratio, feed presence, recency, continuity, sighting volume, temporal
precision, technique attribution. **A signal that measures the record
should contribute the same magnitude and the same sign whatever the lean
is.** A profile declares which kind each signal is; the engine anchors
the lean-side ones and leaves the rest alone.

What that restores, in order:

- **Quality means what §2.3 says again.** A well-corroborated benign
  value scores high because it is well corroborated, not low because it
  is. `127.0.0.1` stops reading as though four organisations reporting it
  were an argument.
- **Thin records get §4's promised state.** `awake-weaves.cyou` becomes
  lean `threat`, quality low, full ledger — *asserted threat, thin
  record* — which is D11 §3's own first test reading.
- **Rule 7 becomes rare and honest.** A negative anchored sum can then
  only come from lean-side evidence, which is exactly the *"record
  disputing its own assertion"* the rule was written for. On the sample
  that is 5 values rather than 60.
- **The contested cases stop lying.** With only lean-side rows anchored,
  a case titled *Reads as benign* contains only rows that read the value
  as benign. §A3 closes with no template change.
- **B1 mostly dissolves.** The two totals stop being the whole ledger
  split by sign, so the difference stops being the quality.

The one thing it does not close is where the quality-only rows go on a
contested layout: they belong to neither case. A third, unopposed strip —
*what the record is worth, either way* — is the shape that fits, and it
is the honest home for the rows §A3 currently mislabels.

### D2. Rule 7 needs its own exit and its own sentence

Whatever §D1 settles, `decided_by` must be rewritten when the lean is.
An eighth exit — the anchored sum going negative — with the sentence
`ValueLeanReasonTool` would write for it. Until then the band under *How
this reading was decided* describes a reading the page discarded (§A4).

### D3. The mechanical five

B2, C1, C2, C3 and C4 are independent of the decision above and can land
in any order. B2 is the only one with a runtime cost, and C5's three
docblocks should be corrected by whoever next touches those files.

Three harness blocks had to be rewritten rather than repaired, because
what they asserted **was** the defect:

- `03`'s fixture replay pre-multiplied the benign ledger by the
  polarity so that anchoring would undo it and arrive at `+91`. That
  arrangement is what let the corpus believe the flip was free. The
  rows now go in as themselves and the assertion is that the lean does
  not touch them — the same ledger sums to 91 under either reading.
- `04` §9.2's benign block, the same thing, plus a new assertion that
  every direction is the authored sign rather than the lean's.
- `03`'s *"a ledger that disputes its lean lands contested, not
  negative"* forced a benign lean on to the malicious demo context and
  expected `contested`, on the reasoning that a record whose every row
  points at a threat cannot be benign. Those rows do not point at a
  threat; they say how much record there is. Replaced by a block that
  builds the contradiction out of lean evidence — a false-positive
  listing under a forced threat lean — and asserts the exit name with
  it.

**Three things this pass deliberately did not do.**

1. **The two-column contested layout is now unreachable on the shipped
   catalogue** — both lean signals argue benign, so no value has a
   threat-side lean row and `casesFor()` always answers empty. The
   templates stay, and a profile carrying a lean signal that argues the
   other way brings them back. What would make them reachable again by
   design is D11 §4's own sentence — *the tug is assertion counts
   against benign evidence* — which needs a rendering for two
   quantities in different units, and §4 leaves that open. Worth
   settling before anyone concludes the layout is dead code.
2. **A negative quality is now visible rather than flipped away.**
   Before, a ledger summing below zero became contested and
   re-anchored positive; now it stays what it is, and
   `awake-weaves.cyou` prints `−1 / 100`. The shipped absence poles
   total `−25` against a `+13` floor for a one-organisation record, so
   the production median lands near or below zero. That is a
   calibration question — the profile owns the points — and it belongs
   with `review-2026-09-02.md` A1 and the `thin_record_clamp` that
   answered it, not with this file.
3. **`attribution.galaxy` stayed on the quality axis** even though its
   docblock argues the absence case as benign evidence. D11 §2.3 names
   *attribution* among quality's sources and §2.1 does not name it
   among the lean's, so the taxonomy settles it; the docblock's
   argument is about a well-populated record and predates the three
   axes. Flagged rather than rewritten.
