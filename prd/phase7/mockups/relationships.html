Recommendation

R1, with two grafts. It is the only one whose structure encodes the thing that is actually true about this tab: the three notions are not three views of one dataset, they have different cardinalities, different failure modes and different empty states. Asserted relationships are always complete and never ranked; co-occurrence is always truncated and must say so; near-matches are small but carry three engine states, one of which is “MISP has no engine for this”. R1 gives each of those its own header, its own cap line and its own empty copy, and nothing in it can be turned into a single blended list by a later change.

Graft one: R3’s faceted pane becomes what R1’s “Open all 18 as a search” opens, so the working table exists without the segmented control’s cost. Graft two: R2’s clustering rule is what R1’s co-occurrence roll-up does past 200 — one node, or one row, per event rather than per value.

Against the others: R2 is the best-looking and the least informative per pixel, and it is an L before it renders anything; R3 risks the exact failure §7.5.3 names, since the asserted segment is the smallest count and the easiest never to click; R4 is sound today but its single grid is the shape most likely to be conflated later, and it is the only one that cannot tell “no correlations” from “no engine applies”.

What MISP cannot supply, in one place

  • Domain / TLD tree relations do not exist. The brief names them as one of the three near-match engines. There is no public-suffix list, no domain-tree table and no code path anywhere in app/Model or app/Lib that computes a parent- or registrable-domain relation. All four candidates draw the gap rather than omitting it.
  • Correlations carry no provenance. The correlations table is value, two event ids, two attribute ids, org and distribution. CIDR and ssdeep matches are written into it by Correlation::__addAdvancedCorrelations() alongside exact matches, with nothing to tell them apart. Splitting co-occurrence from near-match means re-deriving, or adding a column.
  • The ssdeep similarity number is thrown away. ssdeepCorrelation() calls ssdeep_fuzzy_compare(), compares it to MISP.ssdeep_correlation_threshold (40) and keeps only the pair. Showing ssdeep 92% needs a recompute per row and the ssdeep PHP extension present.
  • 21,904 is a hypothetical. MISP.correlation_limit defaults to 20; past it OverCorrelatingValue::block() records the value and MISP stores no correlations for it at all. The honest render of 8.8.8.8 is a suppressed state, not a long list — a fourth state alongside empty, hidden-by-ACL and not-implemented.
  • Analyst relationships are per-attribute, not per-value. Relationship hangs off an object_uuid, so a value-scoped list is the union over the value’s occurrence UUIDs, in both directions, de-duplicated — and a claim on an occurrence you cannot see is invisible to you.
  • There is no value-centred graph feed. CorrelationGraphTool expands events. R2 in particular needs a new node/edge endpoint before it draws anything.
