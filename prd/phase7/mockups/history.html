History — four candidates

This is the one tab whose content is literally an existing MISP view, rendered by an element that already exists (PRD §8.3). So the four candidates disagree only about the organising unit — the stream, the occurrence, the actor, or the changed field — and each has to earn a tab over the seven per-event audit logs the analyst already has.

H1 H2 H3 H4
H1 The shared audit timeline element, value-scoped, with a counted facet rail beside it — the cheapest honest version of this tab.
Optimises for
Landing at zero cost. The body is Logs/timeline.ctp with the entries value-scoped; the rail is phase 8 §5’s facet control.
Gives up
It is a per-event audit log with a different WHERE clause. Nothing in the layout says why a value deserves its own.
At scale
Paginates like the existing index at 1,847 · facet counts need a query the shared element does not have · at zero the whole tab is the not-recorded state.
Data
One union over the value’s occurrences and events, plus per-facet counts — which is the count no MISP endpoint returns over an ACL-scoped set (§7.9, Occurrences).
Size
S — the smallest of the four by a wide margin.

Recommendation

H2, with H1’s facet rail grafted onto it.

H2 is the only candidate that answers the question §8.5 sets as the test. A value-scoped history has to add something to seven per-event ones, and grouping by occurrence is that addition: it shows at a glance that 4831022 has nine entries and 4828810 has two, which no per-event audit log can say because each of them only ever sees one. Its event-level section is also the honest place to put publications, and its footer is the only one of the four that admits the four ACL-hidden occurrences exist and cannot be listed. The graft is small — H1’s rail is phase 8 §5’s facet control, already built — and it gives H2 the filtering it otherwise lacks.

H1 is the right answer to a different question. If this tab has to ship in an afternoon it is the one to build, because it is Logs/timeline.ctp with a WHERE clause. But that is also the case against it: as drawn it is the per-event audit log at a different scope, and §8.5’s test is exactly the one it fails.

H3 is the most interesting and the least buildable. Its whole argument is per-actor attribution, and AuditLogsController::__applyAuditAcl means that for anyone who is not a site admin, three of its four cards collapse to “unnamed users”. A design whose central column is empty for most of its readers is not a design, however good the question. Worth revisiting as a site-admin view, where it is plainly the best of the four.

H4 is rejected on cost against benefit. Unpacking every entry into fields means decoding audit_logs.change for every row at render time, which is the one thing fullChange exists to avoid, and it buys a view that H2 gives most of by expanding one section. Its genuinely good idea — the rename row, called out as the reason scoping is by id and not by title — should be grafted into whichever candidate wins, because it is the §8.2 finding made visible and every candidate needs it.
