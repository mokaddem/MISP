Recommendation

T-final — the composite, drawn after the four were reviewed. It is the fifth frame above and the one this deck recommends.

The four originals each solve one third of the tab and lose the other two. T3 knows when the value was busy and cannot say which source says so. T2 knows which sources have a record and cannot show a single entry. T1 lists the entries and gives the reader no way to find a period. T4 is honest about every fact and has no shape at all. T-final is those four stacked against one selection: brush the spine and the seven lanes re-report, brush it again and the chronology re-lists. Nothing is duplicated because each level answers a different question about the same window — when, then from whom, then what.

It also settles §8.2 in three places rather than one, which is the point of assembling it this way. The two sources MISP can never date keep a full-size hatched lane and a chip on the off-axis strip, so neither a reader who scans the chart nor one who scans the lanes can miss them. The truncated edit lane carries its own hatch, naming the setting that would fill it. And every chronology row states its own precision, so “latest only” sits on the row it qualifies instead of in a caption three panels away.

What it costs is real and should be said plainly: it is the tallest of the five and the most expensive to build — a lane primitive, a brush driving two panels, and three aggregates over one set where T1 needs one list and an existing element. If phase 15 has to be small, T1 with T4’s precision chip is the fallback and remains a defensible tab. T-final is what the tab should be, not the cheapest thing that would pass.

T3 alone stays rejected for the reason it was rejected on its own: a density spine cannot tell a quiet month from an unrecorded one, and on this value December is the second. Inside T-final that objection is answered rather than ignored — the spine no longer stands alone, and the lanes underneath it say which sources were even capable of recording the months it draws.
