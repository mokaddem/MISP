What the ruler said — read 2026-09-02, both grounds

  • Baseline dies at three. Depths 4, 5, 6, 8, 10 and 14 are the same pixel. Source-over is affine — each layer moves the result a fixed fraction of the way to the hue and then there is nowhere left to go — so the paint asymptotes on the hue and every deep pile looks alike. The 50-span lane is one flat bar: it cannot say where the activity is.
  • A separates about twice as far and is the clearest of the three at two or three spans, because the inner span's outline is visible inside the outer one. Two costs: a lone span reads hollow, like a tube rather than a duration, and at 50 the edges are their own noise.
  • B is the only one that reports density. The ramp runs 1 → 14 without flattening in either ground, and on the 50-span lane it draws a real gradient — light where two relations overlap, dark where a dozen do. Multiply is geometric, so it never arrives anywhere and never runs out.
  • The trade is depth-one weight against range, and the sweep below settles it. Because isolation makes the group's backdrop transparent, multiply against it is plain source-over — so depth one is always just this alpha over the card, and at 55% it is pixel-identical to the old paint. But 55% only stretches the readable range from three to about four, which is most of the point thrown away; 22% runs past a dozen and leaves a lone span washed out, and a lone span is the common case, since the value grouping caps a lane at a page of rows and most hold one.
  • Shipped: B at 40%. A lone span keeps close to its old weight, depth separates to about five, and the 50-span lane still locates its dense core. Scoped to .vp-strip and not to .vp-lane-span at large: the Timeline tab draws the same class but its lanes carry several sources at once, each with its own hue, and multiplying two hues invents a third that belongs to neither source. Every span in this strip takes one hue from its caller, so here the blend can only darken.
  • The legend owes the reader a line, and now has one: deeper where spans cover the same dates, with a swatch that stacks two spans through the same rule the lanes use rather than hand-picking a darker fill. It renders only where an overlap is actually drawn — measured in viewBox units through xFor, because two spans a minute apart on a four-year axis are the same pixels.
  • Checked in Chrome only. A's non-scaling-stroke and B's mix-blend-mode inside SVG both want a look in Firefox before either ships.
